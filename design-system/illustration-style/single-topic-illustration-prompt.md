## 1. ARTISTIC STYLE — WATERCOLOR WHISPER

Combine three artistic traditions:

- Delicate, anatomically precise graphite-pencil wildlife drawings.
- Restrained, transparent watercolor applied over visible pencil details.
- The artistic character of beautifully illustrated vintage European natural-history and children's wildlife books.

The resulting illustrations should feel hand-crafted, expressive, warm, timeless, and artistically sophisticated.

### Drawing technique

- Fine, confident pencil lines with subtle variation in pressure and weight.
- Beautifully observed fur, feathers, scales, skin, eyes, and other species-specific details.
- Fine dark accents to define important contours, eyes, noses, and anatomical structures.
- Visible pencil strokes and occasional expressive hatching.
- Watercolor should complement the drawing, not obscure it.
- Use translucent washes, subtle pigment variations, and gently uneven hand-painted fills.
- Avoid excessive detail in unimportant areas.
- Preserve the tactile appearance of a real drawing created on beautiful drawing paper.

The illustration must never look like a photograph processed through a watercolor filter or an overly polished digital painting.

### Color palette

Use the animal's genuine natural coloration.

For environmental elements, use a restrained historical naturalist palette:

- Muted olive and sage green.
- Warm ochre and mustard yellow.
- Gentle rust, sienna, and earthy browns.
- Desaturated slate blue and deep muted teal.
- Warm stone grays.
- Warm light paper matching **sRGB `#F8F5EE`** (RGB 248, 245, 238), the website's light background.

Colors should be harmonious, somewhat restrained, and reminiscent of carefully hand-colored antique zoological illustrations.

Allow occasional stronger color accents, but avoid oversaturation.

### General impression

The animals should appear beautiful, healthy, vibrant, and enjoying their natural lives.

They must convey personality through their authentic expressions and body language—not through cartoon exaggeration or humanized facial features.

## 2. ANATOMICAL ACCURACY — ABSOLUTE PRIORITY

This requirement overrides every stylistic or compositional decision.

Study and respect the actual anatomy and natural movement of [ANIMAL SPECIES].

Ensure:

- Scientifically credible head-to-body proportions.
- Correct species-specific facial structure and expression.
- Accurate ears, eyes, muzzle, beak, mouth, whiskers, horns, or other relevant features.
- Correct length, structure, articulation, and placement of every limb.
- Anatomically accurate joints, paws, toes, claws, hooves, wings, fins, and tails.
- Natural skeletal structure and musculature.
- Age-appropriate differences between adults and juveniles.
- Physically plausible weight distribution, balance, and motion.
- Realistic contact with the ground, water, branches, or other surfaces.

**Never generate:**

- Extra, duplicated, missing, fused, or malformed limbs.
- Additional tails, ears, heads, paws, toes, or other body parts.
- Limbs that emerge from anatomically impossible positions.
- Impossible bends or unnatural joint directions.
- Distorted, elongated, or compressed bodies.
- Anatomically implausible running or jumping poses.
- Artificial human-style smiles or exaggerated expressions.
- Cartoon-like juvenile proportions.

Where limbs overlap or are naturally hidden, their positions must remain physically plausible and consistent with the complete animal.

Avoid complex, extreme movement poses if they would compromise anatomical realism. A simpler but beautiful natural action is always preferable to an impressive-looking but incorrect pose.

**Accuracy is more important than drama.**

## 3. COMPOSITION AND NEGATIVE SPACE

The illustrations must have exceptionally well-balanced compositions.

Study how the supplied references balance detailed animal drawings with generous areas of nearly untouched paper.

### Background philosophy

**The animals receive the detail. The landscape receives the restraint.**

Backgrounds should be artistic, minimal, and evocative rather than comprehensively illustrated.

Use:

- A few carefully placed rocks or rock formations.
- Small, expressive clusters of vegetation.
- Occasional thin branches, grasses, or leaves.
- Simplified distant hills, waterlines, or landforms where appropriate.
- Sparse, confident pencil lines and flat or lightly textured watercolor shapes.
- Deliberately large areas of untouched paper.

A small number of beautifully drawn environmental elements is much better than an abundance of detailed scenery.

The background should communicate a believable natural habitat with the minimum number of visual elements necessary.

### Negative space

Use **sRGB `#F8F5EE`** for the unpainted paper in every image, including all four corners and clear perimeter areas. Do not substitute pure white, gray, a yellow cast, or a different paper tone from the references. Preserve the reference technique in the artwork, but let this exact background color override reference-paper differences. Keep clear paper flat and free of visible texture or gradients so the image edges blend with the light website background; subtle paper character may remain within the drawn/painted areas. Use one opaque asset in both themes, not separate dark-mode variants or transparent backgrounds.

- Leave generous, intentional areas of clear `#F8F5EE` paper.
- Do not fill every empty region with scenery, watercolor washes, texture, or decorative marks.
- Let certain environmental shapes dissolve naturally into untouched paper.
- Background elements may emerge from or continue beyond the image boundaries.
- Avoid uniform rectangular watercolor backgrounds, obvious picture frames, and artificial vignettes.
- Do not add excessive distant mountains, forests, plants, or other environmental details.
- Do not place important animal anatomy against cluttered areas where it becomes difficult to read.

Negative space should feel like a beautiful artistic choice, never like an accidental empty area.

### Subject margins — CRITICAL

Animals must always fit comfortably inside the image.

- Never crop ears, heads, chins, tails, paws, wings, or other essential anatomy accidentally.
- Never allow the subject to touch or nearly touch the lower image boundary.
- Maintain visually balanced breathing room above and below the animal.
- Position animals according to their full silhouettes, including tails and extended limbs.
- Allow sufficient clearance beneath running, jumping, sitting, or playing animals.
- Do not sacrifice anatomical completeness to fill the frame.

Natural cropping is encouraged for environmental elements, **not for the primary animal subjects**.

Avoid both extremes: cramped subjects touching the edges and excessively small subjects surrounded by unnecessary empty space.
