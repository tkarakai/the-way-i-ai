# Privacy

## In short

The Way I AI is a static collection of articles. There are no accounts, no advertising, no cookies, and nothing about you is sold or shared for marketing.

I do use two analytics services, so I know which topics are read and whether pages load quickly. When you open a page, your browser contacts them, and like any web request that reveals your IP address. Neither builds a profile of you or follows you to other websites, and I only see totals, never a record of you as a visitor. The details, and how to opt out, are under [analytics](#analytics).

Fonts, illustrations, and the site’s own scripts come from this site, not from third-party font, image, or script services. The one exception is Cloudflare’s measurement script, described below.

## What your browser remembers

The site uses local storage in your browser to remember things like your chosen design, light or dark theme, animation and keyboard shortcut settings, focus mode, sidebar settings, column widths, reading position, and whether you opted out of analytics.

The theme setting also stores a last-activity time so a manual theme choice can expire after two hours of inactivity. This is used for the display preference, not sent to an analytics service.

These settings stay in your browser. You can remove them through your browser’s site-data controls. Other than the timed theme choice, saved preferences and reading positions remain until cleared or removed by your browser.

## Analytics

Neither service sets cookies or stores anything in your browser. That is also why there is no consent banner: nothing is placed on your device, and no profile is built.

### GoatCounter: what is read

GoatCounter counts page views, so I can see which topics people read, which sites link here, and which links lead to missing pages.

For each page you open, your browser sends GoatCounter the page’s address (without search words or the section you jumped to), its title, your screen size, and the website you came from. The website you came from is sent only if it is another site, and only its address, without search words.

GoatCounter also receives your IP address and browser details, as part of any request. It says it uses them only to recognise repeat views of the same page for up to eight hours, and never stores them. Its servers are in Finland and Germany. See the [GoatCounter privacy policy](https://www.goatcounter.com/privacy).

### Cloudflare Web Analytics: how fast pages load

Cloudflare Web Analytics measures how quickly pages load and respond, so I can find pages that are slow, for example on phones.

Your browser loads a small script from Cloudflare. It reports loading timings, the page’s address, and your browser and device type. Cloudflare also receives your IP address, and uses it to estimate your country. Cloudflare is a US company and may process this data outside the European Union.

Cloudflare says it does not use this data to track visitors across websites, and keeps detailed measurements for seven days before reducing them to a sample. The script is not loaded when a page is opened with search words in its address. See [Cloudflare’s description of Web Analytics](https://developers.cloudflare.com/web-analytics/about/) and the [Cloudflare privacy policy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/).

### Opting out

- **Your browser’s privacy signal:** if it sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, neither service is contacted.
- **This browser only:** open `thewayiai.com/nocount`. The site remembers the choice in your browser and stops contacting both services. Open `thewayiai.com/count` to undo it.
- **Content blockers:** blocking either service does not affect reading.

## Search

Search runs in your browser using a shared index downloaded from this site when you focus or hover over the search button, or open search. There is no external search service.

Search terms can appear in the page URL so they can carry over when you open a result. Those URLs may remain in browser history or be included when you share a link. If you load or reload a URL containing search terms, that URL is sent to the hosting provider as part of the page request.

## Hosting

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. Delivering a page involves sending request information, such as your IP address and the requested URL, to the hosting infrastructure. GitHub may process and retain information under its own policies.

For details, see the [GitHub General Privacy Statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement).
